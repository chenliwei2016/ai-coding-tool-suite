# Tech Writing

Produce clear, structured, actionable technical documentation that people can read, follow, and trace back.

## When to use (when)

- Writing technical solution documents, design documents, review records, or retrospectives
- Writing tutorials / how-to guides / onboarding docs for newcomers
- You need to explain a complex idea clearly to the team

## Input (what to bring in)

- The content to convey / background / target audience
- Relevant decision rationale, steps, and results

## Core steps (process)

1. **Define the reader and purpose first**: Who is it for, and what should they be able to do after reading it — this decides level of detail
2. **"Why → What → How"**: Give background and decision rationale first, then present the structure, and finally the implementation steps
3. **Organize with structure**: Use sections, lists, tables, and code blocks to reduce reading burden
4. **Capture the essence instead of listing everything**: Distill the key assertions and decision rationale; cut redundancy
5. **Make it actionable and traceable**: Provide concrete steps and acceptance criteria, and record the reasons behind key decisions ("why this choice")

## Review/self-check checklist

- [ ] Does the reader finish knowing "why + how"?
- [ ] Is the structure and hierarchy clear, with key points highlighted? (not a bare chronological account)
- [ ] Are the steps actionable, with acceptance criteria?
- [ ] Did you record the rationale behind key decisions (rather than only the conclusions)?

## Common pitfalls

- ❌ Writing only the "what" and omitting the "why" — readers follow it but don't understand the tradeoffs
- ❌ A flat list with no emphasis or hierarchy, reading like a run-on chronology
- ❌ Piling on jargon without considering the reader's perspective
- ❌ Giving only conclusions without rationale, so later readers can't trace the original decision

## Outputs

- Technical documentation that is clearly structured, well-centered on key points, actionable, and traceable
